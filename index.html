How it works

How Cairn turns hand-offs into tested fixes.

Cairn follows six steps, from the first review of your agent's runs to an ongoing check that keeps each fix in place. Your team approves every change.

Why this matters

The hand-offs left over are the expensive part.

Leading support agents now resolve most conversations on their own. The rest go to a person, and in operational workflows each one costs minutes to hours of staff time.

Cairn works on that remainder: it separates the hand-offs your agent could have finished from the ones that should stay with your team.

Security

Your team stays in control.

read-only

Read-only access

Cairn reads your agent's logs and repository. It never writes to them directly.

pull requests

No production changes

Every change arrives as a pull request in your repository.

your approval

Your team merges

Nothing ships until someone on your team approves it.

Why Cairn

Fix what your agent could have done. Keep what it shouldn't.

Chargebacks, sensitive cases and policy approvals belong with your team. Cairn defines this decision boundary explicitly and never automates beyond it.

Before a pilot

Six common questions.

What do you need access to?

Read-only access to your agent's logs and its code repository. Cairn never changes production directly; every change is a pull request.

Does it work with our stack?

Cairn reads the traces you already collect, whatever agent framework or observability tool you use.

What if a fix is wrong?

It's tested on past runs before you see it, and your team approves every merge. Hand-offs that need judgment are never automated.

Do we have to replace our observability tool?

No. Cairn works alongside your existing observability tools.

How is it priced?

A one-time pilot fee, followed by a monthly subscription based on the number of workflows covered.
